# Response to Reviewer (Report 58277902, mca-4499257, Recommendation: Major Revision)

We thank the reviewer for identifying that several claims in the abstract, introduction, and conclusion were stronger than the presented evidence supports. We have revised the manuscript throughout with this concern as the central guide. References are to the revised `main.tex`.

### Major comments

**1. The mathematical definition of the spectral operator is unclear (Hilbert space? Markovian/unitary/non-normal? eigenvalues vs. physical phases? why should a non-unitary dissipative operator's eigenphases correspond to Riemann zeros?).**
We agree the manuscript should not use quantum-mechanical terminology ("unitary evolution," "energy levels") for what is, precisely, a classical Perron–Frobenius-type transfer operator, and have removed the implication of an underlying quantum/unitary structure. §`sec:model` now opens: "We consider a classical, deterministic non-autonomous dynamical system on the interval [-1,1]; no Hilbert-space operator or unitary evolution is postulated or constructed at any point in this work." The operator P is defined concretely as a finite-dimensional, row-stochastic, real (hence non-normal, non-unitary) empirical transition matrix built by Gaussian-kernel discretization (Module A, Methods) and time-averaging (Module B). We do not answer "why should its eigenphases correspond to Riemann zeros" with a mathematical argument, because we do not have one; we instead state plainly (§`sec:rg-caveat`) that we ask "numerically, how closely its eigenphases can be made to track the Riemann zeros... after calibrating a small number of free parameters," and that "we do not claim any isomorphism... between this matrix and the zeros; the correspondence we report is a fitted numerical one." We removed remaining "energy level"/"spectral symmetry" phrasing that implied a physical quantum interpretation is established, replacing e.g. "particle-hole symmetry" with "conjugate symmetry (a consequence of P being a real-valued matrix)" in the Introduction and Module C.

**2. The connection to Hilbert–Pólya is currently overstated; the model is dissipative, discretization-dependent, requires smoothing/optimization/empirical scaling, and does not provide evidence for an underlying Hermitian operator.**
Agreed, and revised accordingly. The title no longer invokes Hilbert–Pólya-style claims (now "Numerical Spectral Correspondence between a Non-Autonomous Quadratic Map and the Riemann Zeros: An Exploratory Study"). The abstract states the results are "numerical observations on a heuristic model, not a proof or Hilbert–Pólya-type operator construction." We adopted the reviewer's suggested three-way distinction directly: the paper's numerical observations are framed as (a) reproducing low-order zeros in-sample (§4.1, explicitly flagged "in-sample only" in Table `tab:claims`) and (b) a fitted mean counting-function trend at large N (§4.2.3–4.2.4), while we explicitly disclaim (c) — "This study takes a different, purely exploratory route... we do not claim any isomorphism" (§`sec:rg-caveat`) and "we do not construct or claim to approximate a self-adjoint Hilbert–Pólya operator" (Introduction). We believe these three categories are no longer mixed, though we have not added an explicit itemized restatement of the distinction at a single location; it is instead maintained consistently at each place a claim is made.

### Minor comments

**1. Introduction excessively long; HP/RMT/quantum-simulation discussion could be more focused.**
Not shortened in this revision. We recognize this is a legitimate readability concern but have prioritized the major methodological and statistical fixes requested by this and other reviewers in the time available; trimming the introduction remains an open item for a subsequent pass.

**2. Speculative physical interpretations should be separated from numerical observations.**
Addressed broadly, primarily via Table `tab:claims` ("Status of the main claims"), which now tabulates the epistemic status — established/published/numerical observation/heuristic/qualitative/refuted/not claimed — of every main statement in the paper, explicitly so that speculative interpretation and numerical observation are not left conflated. In addition, individual speculative passages are now flagged inline, e.g. "we speculate—as a hypothesis, not a demonstrated result—that part of the enlarged deviation..." (§4.1.3) and "we raise—as a hypothesis—the possibility that part of the experimental deviation reflects a single-sided truncation... without asserting it" (§`sec:gue-comparison`).

**3. Figures should include numerical uncertainty estimates.**
Partially addressed. We added explicit run-to-run variability reporting for two of the results most central to the figures: the N=20 residual (20 unselected trials, mean/sd/range reported in §`sec:n20-significance` and referenced from the Figure 2/3 captions) and the Strategy-C k1 optimum (two divergent optimizer runs, both reported, in the Table 1/Figure 4/Figure 5 captions and body text of §`sec:conjugate-ablation`). We have not added formal error bars to every figure (e.g., Figures 6 and 7's macroscopic fits do not carry uncertainty bands), so this remains only partially resolved.

**4. Report computational cost (CPU/GPU, memory).**
Addressed. We added §`sec:repro-guide` ("Reproducibility Guide: Recommended Hardware and Experimental Pipeline") to the Appendix, stating explicitly that no GPU is required anywhere in the study, that all computation is CPU-bound trajectory iteration and sparse eigendecomposition, giving per-experiment core counts and wall-clock times (e.g., "we used a 64-core machine..., completing in ~176 minutes wall-clock" for the out-of-sample study), and RAM requirements ("16 GB... sufficient for every experiment except the N=10,000 ultra-macroscopic transfer matrix, which we recommend running on a machine with at least 32 GB RAM").

**5. Reproducibility should be improved; code availability would help.**
Addressed. The same new §`sec:repro-guide` states all code, notebooks, and pre-computed outputs are available at the linked repository, lists the software dependencies and versions (Python 3.12, numpy/scipy/numba/mpmath/matplotlib), and gives a numbered list of the main experimental steps in the order results appear in the paper, each naming the specific script/notebook that reproduces it. The Data Availability Statement at the end of the manuscript points to the same repository and to the standard high-precision zero tabulation (`mpmath.zetazero`) used for benchmarking.

**6. Conclusion should avoid suggesting a possible solution to Hilbert–Pólya.**
Addressed. The Outlook subsection (§Discussion) now frames the work only as being "of interest alongside the Berry–Keating conjecture as an alternative, dissipative-dynamics numerical experiment," and any forward-looking suggestion (e.g., about symmetric hardware evolution reducing dispersion) is explicitly marked "a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated prescription." No language in the current conclusion claims or implies a solution or partial solution to the Hilbert–Pólya problem; Table `tab:claims`'s final row states plainly, "Operator spectrum equals the Riemann zeros → Not claimed (conjectural)."

### Summary
Both major comments and four of six minor comments are substantially addressed; the introduction-length concern (Minor 1) and full per-figure uncertainty bands (Minor 3, partially) remain open for a future revision.
