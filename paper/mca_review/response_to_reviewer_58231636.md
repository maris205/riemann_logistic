# Response to Reviewer (Report 58231636, Decision: Reject)

We are grateful for this thorough and rigorous review. Several of the issues raised are ones we cannot fully resolve within the scope of a numerical, exploratory study, and we say so plainly below rather than overclaiming a fix. Others — particularly the Table 1/Figure 4–5 inconsistency (Major Comment 6) — pointed us to a genuine error that we have now corrected and disclosed. References are to the revised `main.tex`.

### Major Comments

**1. Meaning of "spectral isomorphism" / Hilbert–Pólya connection.**
Accepted as a structural framing problem, not fully resolved as a mathematical one — because it cannot be: no unitary operator U or self-adjoint Hilbert–Pólya realization is constructed anywhere in this work, and we have removed the implication that one is. The title no longer contains "isomorphism" (it now reads "Numerical Spectral Correspondence... An Exploratory Study"), and §`sec:model` states explicitly: "We consider a classical, deterministic non-autonomous dynamical system on the interval [-1,1]; no Hilbert-space operator or unitary evolution is postulated or constructed at any point in this work." §`sec:rg-caveat` similarly states: "We do not claim any isomorphism, in the precise mathematical sense of a structure-preserving bijection between independently defined objects, between this matrix and the zeros; the correspondence we report is a fitted numerical one." We agree with the reviewer that this leaves the manuscript without a Hilbert–Pólya-type result — that is now the explicit, stated position of the paper rather than an aspiration it fails to meet.

**2. 1/log²n law is heuristic, not rigorous.**
Fully agreed and revised, per our response to a parallel comment from another reviewer: the relevant subsection is retitled "Heuristic Motivation for the Scaling Law," and every place that previously implied the form was "strictly dictated" or that alternatives were "invalid" has been softened to state the chain "motivates, but does not derive from first principles" the leading-order form, and that we "do not claim this constitutes a proof that such alternative forms are excluded on independent theoretical grounds."

**3. Target leakage / no genuine out-of-sample prediction.**
This is the most consequential comment, and we agree with it. We added §`sec:oos`, "Out-of-Sample Validation and Multi-Seed Robustness": fitting k1 on the first M∈{50,70,80} zeros and evaluating on the held-out remainder gives held-out MSE one to two orders of magnitude larger than training MSE (e.g., M=70: train MSE=146.0 vs. test MSE=5891), and the fitted k1 itself drifts with M (5.79→9.15→10.04) rather than converging. We state the conclusion the reviewer is asking for directly: "the model is a calibrated in-sample reconstruction over the zeros it was fitted to, not a predictor of unseen zeros, and we do not claim otherwise." We have not extended this holdout protocol to the microscopic ε-selection step (Eq. 6) or to the N=1000/10000 regimes; those remain in-sample by the reviewer's same standard, and the abstract and Table `tab:claims` are written to reflect that scope limitation rather than to imply it has been resolved everywhere.

**4. Non-autonomous transfer operator: time-averaging vs. ordered product; Pij normalization.**
Both sub-points are now addressed directly, though the first only by disclosure, not by resolution. On the time-average: §Module B (Methods) now states explicitly that "for a non-autonomous system, the evolution over T steps is governed by the time-ordered product P_{T-1}...P_0, and in general the spectrum of this ordered product is unrelated to the spectrum of the arithmetic mean P̄_T," and, "We do not provide an adiabatic theorem, ergodic argument, or convergence proof that would justify treating P̄_T as equivalent to the ordered-product spectrum; we instead treat P̄_T as a computationally tractable heuristic surrogate." This is an honest concession that the reviewer's concern is correct and unresolved as a matter of theory; we motivate the surrogate only heuristically (slow parameter drift relative to mixing time), not by proof. On Pij normalization: we confirm the reviewer is right that the un-normalized joint frequency does not row-sum to 1. Equation~\eqref{eq:pij} in the current manuscript is stated with the correct conditional-frequency normalization (division by the source-bin occupation frequency), and we added a note clarifying that "the implementation used to generate all numerical results reported below always applies the row normalization shown here, and the discrepancy was confined to the typeset equation, not the underlying code" — i.e., this was a transcription error in an earlier draft's equation, not in the actual computation, and it is now fixed in both the equation and this disclosure.

**5. Interpretation of the conjugate full-spectrum fit (vanishing intercept).**
Agreed with the reviewer's algebra, and now stated as such rather than disputed. §`sec:conjugate-ablation` states: "it follows directly from the normal equations of ordinary least squares that the unconstrained intercept must satisfy b=0; this part of the result is therefore an algebraic consequence of the symmetric construction, not an independent finding about the dynamics." We separate this from what we now argue is a genuine (not purely algebraic) observation: the fitted slope k1 differs between Strategy C (6.48) and Strategy D (4.39) because k1 is independently re-optimized per strategy and enters the map before the transfer matrix is constructed — Strategy D's positive-branch eigenphases are therefore a different numerical object from Strategy C's, not a mirrored copy of the same data under a different regression. We also explicitly concede the reviewer's implicit counterfactual: had k1 been held fixed and only the regression symmetrized, the intercept-zero slope and the free-fit slope on the symmetric data would coincide exactly, by the same normal-equations logic used for b=0.

**6. Table 1 vs. Figure 4 vs. Figure 5 inconsistency.**
This is a genuine defect that we traced to its root cause and disclose rather than silently reconcile. The two sets of numbers come from two separate runs of the same unseeded `scipy.optimize.differential_evolution` call (no `seed` argument, `workers=-1`, parallel population search) for Strategy C's k1 optimization; because the objective is non-convex and multimodal in k1, the two runs converged to different local optima (k1≈6.481, MSE≈8.48 in one run; k1≈8.070, MSE≈10.08 in the other), and each run's numbers were used for a different figure/table without our having recognized this at the time. We have not "fixed" this by picking one canonical number, since doing so would misrepresent that the optimizer is not currently reproducible; instead, Table 1's caption, the Figure 4 caption, and the body text preceding Figure 4 (§`sec:conjugate-ablation`) all now state explicitly that this is "genuine run-to-run optimizer instability, not evidence of two distinct experimental protocols," report both (k1, MSE) pairs, and state which run each figure uses. We added a corresponding row to Table `tab:claims`: "Macroscopic k1 optimum is reproducible run-to-run (Strategy C) → Refuted." We regard eliminating this instability (e.g., by fixing a seed, using a single worker, or switching to a deterministic bounded optimizer as we did for the §`sec:oos` study) as a natural next step, but have chosen to disclose the instability honestly in this revision rather than quietly re-running until we got matching numbers, which would not address the underlying multimodality. As independent, quantitative corroboration that the underlying objective is genuinely multimodal rather than an artifact of these two specific runs, we reran the identical optimizer configuration at a reduced, computationally tractable scale (2×10⁷ steps vs. 10¹⁰, smaller population) under five explicit fixed seeds (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 4): the fitted k1 ranged from 9.33 to 14.26 (mean 12.43, sd 1.74) across the five seeds. We are explicit in the manuscript that these reduced-scale k1 values are not numerically comparable to the 6.481/8.070 pair, since reducing the step count changes the objective itself rather than only its cost — this experiment supports the general diagnosis (an unseeded population-based optimizer on this objective class will not reproduce a single answer run-to-run), not a specific reconciliation of the two original numbers.

**7. Contradictory large-N scaling protocol description (Results says single-point anchoring only; Appendix mentions multi-point/piecewise scaling).**
We have reviewed this and believe the apparent contradiction is a scope confusion that the manuscript should state more clearly, though we have only partially fixed it. §`sec:spectral-fitting-methodology` (Methods) now states explicitly: "We use this strict single-point anchoring throughout, including for the N=1000 dynamical fits (M1, M2) reported in Section~\ref{sec:macroscopic}... The only exception is the GUE random-matrix baseline, which... is instead calibrated with a global least-squares scaling forced through the origin." The piecewise Shift-and-Invert method described in Appendix F.3 is a separate technique for a different, larger regime (N>10,000, used to extract eigenvalue *bands* without scanning the full spectrum) and does not change how the scaling constant a is determined once eigenphases are obtained — but the manuscript does not yet cross-reference this explicitly at the point the reviewer would read it (Appendix F.3 itself), so we acknowledge the two sections read as contradictory without that added cross-reference and have not yet added it.

**8. GUE baseline is not a like-for-like test.**
Fixed. §`sec:gue-comparison` now states the globally-rescaled-GUE-vs-counting-function result "is expected and is *not* a refutation of random-matrix theory," and adds the like-for-like test the reviewer requests: we unfolded our own model's eigenphases and the true zeros and ran a KS test against GUE and Poisson nearest-neighbor spacing distributions. Result: the true zeros match GUE (D=0.067, p=0.74) as expected; our model's own eigenphases do not (D=0.300, p<10⁻⁴) and are statistically indistinguishable from Poisson (D=0.082, p=0.49). We report this as "a genuine limitation of the numerical correspondence that we report directly rather than omit," concluding that our numerical match concerns mean/counting-function behavior, not the fine-grained local statistics that are the actual established GUE property.

**9. Post-hoc ion-trap comparison lacks a null distribution/significance test.**
Fixed with the significance testing described above in our response to Reviewer 58231421 Comment 3: §`sec:n20-significance` reports a Spearman rank correlation (ρ=0.56, p=0.010) and a 100,000-resample permutation test for the joint top-rank coincidence specifically at N=20 (p=0.099). We present both figures and do not claim the joint coincidence is established at conventional significance; the text states "we reiterate that even a significant rank coincidence would establish a shared statistical pattern, not a shared physical mechanism."

**10. Spectral filtering/unwrapping choices need sensitivity analysis.**
Addressed with a new sensitivity sweep (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 1), and the result is that the procedure is considerably less robust than the previous round's disclosure implied. Holding one empirical matrix fixed (the Strategy-C construction at k1=6.481, 10¹⁰ steps), we varied the magnitude cutoff (0.2–0.6), the conjugate-branch selection (upper half-plane, lower half-plane, or folded), and whether the filtered phases are sorted before unwrapping. The baseline configuration used in the main text (cutoff 0.4, upper half-plane, sorted) gives MSE≈288 against the comparison set used in this sweep; switching only the sort-before-unwrap step to "unsorted" inflates the MSE by roughly 20–40× and can flip the sign of the fitted slope, and switching only the branch to the lower half-plane produces a comparably large degradation. The cutoff value itself matters far less over the tested range than the ordering and branch choices do. Module C's existing disclosure ("we have not established its stability...") is now followed by these concrete numbers, and we no longer describe this only as an unverified assumption — it is now a verified, and substantial, robustness failure that we report as such rather than downplay.

**11. Numerical sensitivity (10⁻¹⁵ perturbations) conflicts with robustness claims.**
Partially addressed. We did add multi-seed robustness testing for the macroscopic k1 fit (§`sec:oos`: 10 seeds, CV≈4.7%) and repeated-trial testing for the ARPACK eigensolver (§`sec:n20-significance`: 20 trials). We have not, however, run the specific floating-point/compiler-sensitivity test the reviewer asks for (reproducing across compiler settings/precision levels for the full 10¹⁰-step macroscopic trajectories) — Appendix F.1 documents the sensitivity as a known issue and recommends a mitigation (isolating boundary-anchor constants in IEEE-754-compliant code), but does not report a systematic sweep across settings; given the other findings below, we have prioritized limited follow-up effort there over this specific sweep and still regard it as open. This, combined with the unseeded-DE instability disclosed above (Comment 6, now with independent multi-seed corroboration) and the new spectral-filtering sensitivity result (Comment 10), means the reviewer's underlying concern — that a single highly sensitive trajectory cannot support strong robustness claims — is one we now substantiate with concrete numbers rather than dispute, and Table `tab:claims` reflects this by marking the relevant claims as run-to-run variable or refuted rather than established.

**12. Critical-point terminology inconsistency (1.5437 baseline vs. "Feigenbaum critical point").**
Fixed. We checked all occurrences of 1.543689/1.5437 in the current text; each is now consistently called the "empirically anchored baseline" or "critical baseline," explicitly distinguished from the Feigenbaum accumulation point (≈1.401) at both places it is introduced (§`sec:model` and Appendix). We did not find a remaining instance calling 1.543689 the "Feigenbaum critical point" in the current draft; if the reviewer can point to a specific remaining line in a future round we will fix it immediately.

### Decisive Reasons for Rejection

These restate Major Comments 1–3, 5, 6, and 8 as the paper's core defects. We do not believe a numerical, exploratory study of this kind can fully overcome reasons 1–3 (no self-adjoint operator is or can be constructed here; the fit is inherently target-calibrated) — the revised manuscript's response is to state these limitations as the paper's explicit, acknowledged scope rather than to claim they are resolved. Reasons 5 and 6 (algebraic intercept, Table/Figure inconsistency) are corrected/disclosed as described above. Reason 8 (missing uncertainty budget/out-of-sample test/seed variability) is now substantially, though not completely, addressed via §`sec:oos` and §`sec:n20-significance`; the compiler/precision sensitivity sweep specifically remains outstanding, per our response to Comment 11.

We recognize that, taken together, these limitations may still fall short of what would be required for publication as originally framed; the revision's strategy has been to make the paper's actual, more modest evidentiary status transparent (see Table `tab:claims`) rather than to argue the original framing was correct.
