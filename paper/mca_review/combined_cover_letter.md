# Cover Letter — Resubmission of Manuscript mca-4536128

**Manuscript ID:** mca-4536128 (previously mca-4499257)
**Title (revised):** "Numerical Spectral Correspondence between a Non-Autonomous Quadratic Map and the Riemann Zeros: An Exploratory Study"
**Journal:** Mathematical and Computational Applications (MCA)
**Author:** Liang Wang

Dear Editor,

Thank you for the opportunity to revise and resubmit this manuscript. We received four
detailed reviewer reports, three recommending rejection and one major revision. We have
made extensive changes throughout the manuscript in direct response to every itemized
comment, and we attach one point-by-point response letter per reviewer report
(`response_to_reviewer_58231421.md`, `response_to_reviewer_58231636.md`,
`response_to_reviewer_58277902.md`, `response_to_reviewer_58279672.md`).

Rather than argue against the reviewers' central assessment, our revision strategy has
been to accept it: this is a numerical, exploratory study that cannot establish a
Hilbert–Pólya-type operator or a genuinely predictive fit, and several of the original
draft's claims were overstated relative to what the numerics actually show. We have
rewritten the manuscript to state its actual, more modest evidentiary status explicitly
and consistently — including reporting new negative/unfavorable results discovered while
investigating the reviewers' concerns — rather than to defend the original framing.

## Summary of the main changes

**1. Title, abstract, and framing.** The title no longer claims a "spectral isomorphism"
or "renormalization flow"; it now reads as above and the abstract states the results are
"numerical observations on a heuristic model, not a proof or Hilbert–Pólya-type operator
construction." Rhetorical/metaphorical language flagged by reviewers (e.g. "ocean of
chaos," "topological fossil," "spontaneous calibrator," "Linear Homomorphic Mapping,"
"particle-hole symmetry," "global cooling inertia," "Topology Maintenance") has been
removed throughout and replaced with plain technical language.

**2. New epistemic status table (Table "Status of the main claims").** Every main claim in the
paper is now tagged with its actual epistemic status (established/published, numerical
observation, heuristic, qualitative, conjectural, refuted, or not claimed), including two
entries we mark **Refuted**: out-of-sample extrapolation of the fitted k1, and a match to
GUE local (unfolded) spacing statistics.

**3. Out-of-sample validation and multi-seed robustness (new §`sec:oos`).** We added a
genuine held-out test: fitting k1 on the first M∈{50,70,80} zeros and evaluating on the
rest. Held-out MSE is one to two orders of magnitude larger than training MSE at every M,
and we state directly that "the model is a calibrated in-sample reconstruction... not a
predictor of unseen zeros." A ten-seed robustness check at fixed M=70 is also reported.

**4. Statistical significance testing for the N=20 / USTC coincidence (new
§`sec:n20-significance`).** The previously cherry-picked N=20 residual spike is now
reported from 20 unselected eigendecomposition trials, plus a Spearman rank correlation
and a 100,000-resample permutation test for the specific joint-rank coincidence
(p=0.099, not significant at the conventional threshold).

**5. Genuine like-for-like GUE/RMT test (§`sec:gue-comparison`).** We unfolded our
model's own eigenphases and ran a KS test against GUE and Poisson spacing distributions;
unlike the true Riemann zeros (which do match GUE, as expected from the literature), our
model's own eigenphases do **not** match GUE and are statistically indistinguishable from
Poisson. We report this directly as a limitation rather than omitting it.

**6. Disclosure and root-cause tracing of the Table 1 / Figure 4–5 numerical
inconsistency.** We traced the discrepancy between k1≈6.481 (Table 1) and k1≈8.070
(Figures 4–5) to genuine run-to-run instability of an *unseeded* `differential_evolution`
call on a non-convex, multimodal objective — not two different experimental protocols.
Both values are now reported explicitly in the relevant captions, and Table `tab:claims`
carries a corresponding "Refuted" row for run-to-run reproducibility of this optimum.

**7. Four new targeted numerical experiments, added in this final revision round
(new §`sec:post-review-checks`), directly answering four specific reviewer requests for
numerical evidence rather than qualitative acknowledgment.** All four experiments'
code and raw output are included in the submission (`review_experiments/` in the
repository). We report all four results as-is, including where they are unfavorable:
   - *Phase-extraction/Module-C sensitivity*: varying only the eigenvalue magnitude
     cutoff, conjugate-branch selection, and sort-before-unwrap convention on one fixed
     empirical matrix changes the fit MSE by roughly 20–40×, and can flip the fitted
     slope's sign. This confirms, with numbers, a sensitivity we had previously only
     flagged as an unverified assumption.
   - *Gaussian-splatting vs. Monte Carlo overlap*: under matched parameters and a shared
     eigenvalue filter, the two transfer-matrix construction methods retained only 3
     directly-comparable eigenvalues, over which their independently fitted slopes
     differed by roughly 35×. This is a negative/inconclusive result, not the
     consistency check we had hoped to run.
   - *Epsilon out-of-sample split*: selecting the microscopic discretization scale ε on
     half of the first 6 zeros and testing on the other half shows the held-out error is
     roughly 5× larger under an asymmetric split than when ε is chosen with knowledge of
     all 6 zeros — concrete, quantitative target-leakage evidence, on the same footing as
     the existing k1 out-of-sample result.
   - *Multi-seed DE robustness at reduced scale*: rerunning the same optimizer
     configuration under five explicit fixed seeds (at a computationally tractable
     reduced scale) shows k1 ranging from 9.33 to 14.26 (std 1.74), independently
     confirming that the objective is genuinely multimodal rather than a one-off
     discrepancy between the two full-scale runs behind Table 1 and Figures 4–5.

   We want to be transparent with the editor and reviewers that these four experiments
   were designed to test open concerns, and three of the four returned results that
   reinforce rather than resolve the underlying limitations. We report them honestly
   rather than omit or reframe them, consistent with the overall revision strategy above.

**8. Terminology and mathematical corrections.** We fixed a transcription error in the
row-normalization equation for the empirical transition matrix (the implementation was
always correct; only the typeset equation was wrong in the previous draft), corrected
"Feigenbaum critical point" terminology, and removed remaining anthropomorphic/quantum
terminology not warranted by the classical, non-unitary construction actually used.

**9. Reproducibility.** A new Appendix subsection ("Reproducibility Guide: Recommended
Hardware and Experimental Pipeline") and an updated repository `readme.md` give explicit
hardware/runtime requirements and map every numerical claim in the paper to a specific,
runnable script, all of which (including the four new post-review-check scripts) are
released with pre-computed output alongside this submission.

## What remains open

Consistent with our stated revision strategy, several concerns raised by reviewers are
**not** claimed to be resolved: a theoretical justification for treating the
time-averaged empirical matrix's spectrum as representative of the true time-ordered
product's spectrum (raised independently by two reviewers) remains an open, unproven
heuristic; target leakage has not been eliminated for μc or for the separately-refit
N=1000/10000 regimes; and a systematic floating-point/compiler-precision sensitivity
sweep for the full 10¹⁰-step trajectories has not been run. Each response letter states
these limitations explicitly rather than asserting a fix we have not verified.

We believe the manuscript, as revised, now presents an honest and considerably more
carefully hedged account of what this numerical exploration does and does not show, and
we thank the reviewers for pushing the work toward that more accurate framing. We hope
the revised manuscript, together with the attached point-by-point responses, addresses
the Editorial Office's concerns sufficiently for further consideration.

Sincerely,
Liang Wang

# Appendix A: Response to Reviewer (Report 58231421)


We thank the reviewer for a detailed, constructive reading. We address each comment in turn; line/section references are to the revised `main.tex`.

**Comment 1 (1/ln²n scaling law is heuristic, not rigorous).**
Agreed, and revised. The subsection is retitled "Heuristic Motivation for the Scaling Law" (was "Theoretical Derivation..."), §Methods, `\label{sec:scaling-derivation}`. We removed language claiming the form is "strictly dictated" or that alternative forms are "ruled out," and now state explicitly: "The following chain of heuristic correspondences motivates, but does not derive from first principles, a particular form..." and, after the derivation, "We do not claim this constitutes a proof that such alternative forms are excluded on independent theoretical grounds, only that 1/ln²n performs better empirically among the forms we tried." The companion subsection (previously "Topological Constraints on the Direction of Evolution") is retitled "Choice of Direction of Evolution" with the same softening. Section 2.2's forward-reference was updated to match ("we do not derive it from a theorem... and we do not claim that alternative logarithmic forms are excluded").

**Comment 2 (overfitting risk from multiple free parameters ε, μ_c, k1, k2 tuned to low-lying zeros).**
Addressed, including the ε holdout test the previous round left open, and the result confirms the reviewer's concern rather than allaying it. To clarify the premise: in the microscopic (N≤20) pipeline that Eq. 6 (ErrSum) governs, only ε is a fitted parameter — μ_c is not fitted but anchored directly to the first zero via k = γ1/θ1 (a single-point, zero-degree-of-freedom rescaling, Module C), and k1, k2 do not enter this microscopic construction at all (they appear only in the separate N=100/1000/10000 macroscopic protocols). We had already added an out-of-sample/multi-seed robustness study for the macroscopic k1 fit (§`sec:oos`): fitting on the first M∈{50,70,80} zeros and testing on the rest gives held-out MSE one to two orders of magnitude larger than training MSE. We have now run the analogous holdout test for ε itself (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 3, reduced-cost pipeline at 2×10⁵ steps): splitting the first 6 zeros into a training half and a held-out half, training ε on the first 3 zeros and testing on the last 3 gives a held-out error sum of 28.0, versus 5.4 when ε is instead selected using all 6 zeros (the protocol used to report the fit quality in the main text) and evaluated on that same held-out set — a roughly 5× degradation. (A second, odd/even split happened to select the same ε as the full-sample optimum and was therefore uninformative about leakage; we report both splits rather than only the one that shows the effect.) We now report this directly as target-leakage evidence for ε, on the same footing as the existing k1 out-of-sample result, rather than as an open item.

**Comment 3 (USTC comparison statistically underdeveloped).**
Fixed. We added §`sec:n20-significance` ("Statistical Significance of the USTC Coincidence"): the N=20 residual is now reported from 20 unselected eigendecomposition trials (mean 11.76, sd 1.59, range 10.93–16.50, rather than a single cherry-picked value), and we run a Spearman rank correlation (ρ=0.56, p=0.010) and a 100,000-resample permutation test for the specific joint-top-rank coincidence at N=20 (p=0.099, not significant at the conventional threshold). We report both numbers and state explicitly that even a significant rank correlation "would establish a shared statistical pattern, not a shared physical mechanism." We have not moved this material out of the main text into a separate speculative section as suggested, since the statistical testing itself now supports keeping it in the main results with appropriate hedging, but we did shift the framing throughout (Fig. 3 caption, §4.3 "Error Composition") to state up front that the two error sources are physically distinct before any coincidence is mentioned.

**Comment 4 (GUE baseline framing could mislead).**
Fixed. §`sec:gue-comparison` now states directly: "This is expected and is *not* a refutation of random-matrix theory: the established GUE correspondence concerns the *unfolded local* statistics of the zeros..., not a claim that a single globally scaled spectrum reproduces the logarithmically growing counting function." We also added a genuine like-for-like RMT test: we unfolded the transfer matrix's own eigenphases and ran a KS test against GUE and Poisson spacing distributions, finding (as a literature sanity check) the true zeros match GUE (D=0.067, p=0.74) but our model's own eigenphases do not (D=0.300, p<10⁻⁴) and are statistically indistinguishable from Poisson. We report this directly as "a genuine limitation of the numerical correspondence."

**Comment 5 (undemonstrated consistency between Gaussian-splatting and Monte Carlo constructions across N=20/N=100).**
Addressed with a new numerical overlap check (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 2), and the outcome is a negative result rather than the validation we had hoped for. We built both constructions under identical physical parameters and identical, modest step/grid budgets (2×10⁵ steps, 1000 bins, chosen to keep the much more expensive Gaussian-splatting construction tractable) and applied the same Module C eigenvalue filter to both. The Monte Carlo matrix retained 82 eigenvalues satisfying the filter; the Gaussian-splatting matrix retained only 3, so the directly comparable range was limited to N=1–3. Over that narrow shared range, the two methods' independently forced-origin-fitted slopes differed by roughly 35× (348 vs. 10). This does not demonstrate that the two constructions are consistent; at this scale it shows the opposite, though we cannot rule out that the discrepancy is an artifact of the small step/grid budget the splatting method's cost forced on us rather than a genuine inconsistency at scale. We have revised §`sec:conjugate-ablation`'s intro accordingly, so it no longer implies an unstated equivalence: it now states explicitly that "this is a change of method, not a validated equivalence between methods" and points to this negative overlap result rather than describing the macroscopic protocol as a natural extension of the microscopic one.

**Comment 6 (vanishing intercept for Strategy D presented as trivial without dynamical content).**
Fixed, with a specific answer to "what dynamical content this observation is meant to convey." §`sec:conjugate-ablation` now states explicitly that b=0 "is therefore an algebraic consequence of the symmetric construction, not an independent finding about the dynamics," and separately identifies what is *not* forced: the fitted slope k1 changes (6.48→4.39) and the residual envelope shrinks (~2.8%→~1.5%) because k1 is independently re-optimized per strategy and enters the map before P is built — so Strategy D's eigenphases are a genuinely different numerical object, not a mirrored copy of Strategy C's. We also added the converse observation for full transparency: had k1 been held fixed across strategies and only the regression symmetrized, the intercept-zero slope and the free-fit slope on the symmetrized data would coincide exactly by the same normal-equations argument — we did not do that here, which is why the slopes differ.

**Comment 7 (RG interpretation of k1 shift under-justified).**
Fixed. §`sec:rg-caveat` ("Macroscopic Behavior of the Empirical Transfer-Matrix Spectrum") now states: "we do not identify grid resolution with a renormalization-group ultraviolet cutoff, and we do not claim that this drift constitutes evidence of a renormalization-group flow in the dynamical sense (no beta function, fixed point, or universality result is established here)." The parameter shift is now presented purely as "an empirical observation about how the fit changes with grid resolution and range," with explicit acknowledgment that "we cannot rule out that some part of this shift reflects overfitting to the specific fitting range used at each scale."

**Comment 8 (rhetorical language exceeding the modest scope of the findings).**

# Appendix B: Response to Reviewer (Report 58231636)


We are grateful for this thorough and rigorous review. Several of the issues raised are ones we cannot fully resolve within the scope of a numerical, exploratory study, and we say so plainly below rather than overclaiming a fix. Others — particularly the Table 1/Figure 4–5 inconsistency (Major Comment 6) — pointed us to a genuine error that we have now corrected and disclosed. References are to the revised `main.tex`.

### Major Comments

**1. Meaning of "spectral isomorphism" / Hilbert–Pólya connection.**
Accepted as a structural framing problem, not fully resolved as a mathematical one — because it cannot be: no unitary operator U or self-adjoint Hilbert–Pólya realization is constructed anywhere in this work, and we have removed the implication that one is. The title no longer contains "isomorphism" (it now reads "Numerical Spectral Correspondence... An Exploratory Study"), and §`sec:model` states explicitly: "We consider a classical, deterministic non-autonomous dynamical system on the interval [-1,1]; no Hilbert-space operator or unitary evolution is postulated or constructed at any point in this work." §`sec:rg-caveat` similarly states: "We do not claim any isomorphism, in the precise mathematical sense of a structure-preserving bijection between independently defined objects, between this matrix and the zeros; the correspondence we report is a fitted numerical one." We agree with the reviewer that this leaves the manuscript without a Hilbert–Pólya-type result — that is now the explicit, stated position of the paper rather than an aspiration it fails to meet.

**2. 1/log²n law is heuristic, not rigorous.**
Fully agreed and revised, per our response to a parallel comment from another reviewer: the relevant subsection is retitled "Heuristic Motivation for the Scaling Law," and every place that previously implied the form was "strictly dictated" or that alternatives were "invalid" has been softened to state the chain "motivates, but does not derive from first principles" the leading-order form, and that we "do not claim this constitutes a proof that such alternative forms are excluded on independent theoretical grounds."

**3. Target leakage / no genuine out-of-sample prediction.**
This is the most consequential comment, and we agree with it. We added §`sec:oos`, "Out-of-Sample Validation and Multi-Seed Robustness": fitting k1 on the first M∈{50,70,80} zeros and evaluating on the held-out remainder gives held-out MSE one to two orders of magnitude larger than training MSE (e.g., M=70: train MSE=146.0 vs. test MSE=5891), and the fitted k1 itself drifts with M (5.79→9.15→10.04) rather than converging. We state the conclusion the reviewer is asking for directly: "the model is a calibrated in-sample reconstruction over the zeros it was fitted to, not a predictor of unseen zeros, and we do not claim otherwise." We have not extended this holdout protocol to the microscopic ε-selection step (Eq. 6) or to the N=1000/10000 regimes; those remain in-sample by the reviewer's same standard, and the abstract and Table `tab:claims` are written to reflect that scope limitation rather than to imply it has been resolved everywhere.

**4. Non-autonomous transfer operator: time-averaging vs. ordered product; Pij normalization.**
Both sub-points are now addressed directly, though the first only by disclosure, not by resolution. On the time-average: §Module B (Methods) now states explicitly that "for a non-autonomous system, the evolution over T steps is governed by the time-ordered product P_{T-1}...P_0, and in general the spectrum of this ordered product is unrelated to the spectrum of the arithmetic mean P̄_T," and, "We do not provide an adiabatic theorem, ergodic argument, or convergence proof that would justify treating P̄_T as equivalent to the ordered-product spectrum; we instead treat P̄_T as a computationally tractable heuristic surrogate." This is an honest concession that the reviewer's concern is correct and unresolved as a matter of theory; we motivate the surrogate only heuristically (slow parameter drift relative to mixing time), not by proof. On Pij normalization: we confirm the reviewer is right that the un-normalized joint frequency does not row-sum to 1. Equation~\eqref{eq:pij} in the current manuscript is stated with the correct conditional-frequency normalization (division by the source-bin occupation frequency), and we added a note clarifying that "the implementation used to generate all numerical results reported below always applies the row normalization shown here, and the discrepancy was confined to the typeset equation, not the underlying code" — i.e., this was a transcription error in an earlier draft's equation, not in the actual computation, and it is now fixed in both the equation and this disclosure.

**5. Interpretation of the conjugate full-spectrum fit (vanishing intercept).**
Agreed with the reviewer's algebra, and now stated as such rather than disputed. §`sec:conjugate-ablation` states: "it follows directly from the normal equations of ordinary least squares that the unconstrained intercept must satisfy b=0; this part of the result is therefore an algebraic consequence of the symmetric construction, not an independent finding about the dynamics." We separate this from what we now argue is a genuine (not purely algebraic) observation: the fitted slope k1 differs between Strategy C (6.48) and Strategy D (4.39) because k1 is independently re-optimized per strategy and enters the map before the transfer matrix is constructed — Strategy D's positive-branch eigenphases are therefore a different numerical object from Strategy C's, not a mirrored copy of the same data under a different regression. We also explicitly concede the reviewer's implicit counterfactual: had k1 been held fixed and only the regression symmetrized, the intercept-zero slope and the free-fit slope on the symmetric data would coincide exactly, by the same normal-equations logic used for b=0.

**6. Table 1 vs. Figure 4 vs. Figure 5 inconsistency.**
This is a genuine defect that we traced to its root cause and disclose rather than silently reconcile. The two sets of numbers come from two separate runs of the same unseeded `scipy.optimize.differential_evolution` call (no `seed` argument, `workers=-1`, parallel population search) for Strategy C's k1 optimization; because the objective is non-convex and multimodal in k1, the two runs converged to different local optima (k1≈6.481, MSE≈8.48 in one run; k1≈8.070, MSE≈10.08 in the other), and each run's numbers were used for a different figure/table without our having recognized this at the time. We have not "fixed" this by picking one canonical number, since doing so would misrepresent that the optimizer is not currently reproducible; instead, Table 1's caption, the Figure 4 caption, and the body text preceding Figure 4 (§`sec:conjugate-ablation`) all now state explicitly that this is "genuine run-to-run optimizer instability, not evidence of two distinct experimental protocols," report both (k1, MSE) pairs, and state which run each figure uses. We added a corresponding row to Table `tab:claims`: "Macroscopic k1 optimum is reproducible run-to-run (Strategy C) → Refuted." We regard eliminating this instability (e.g., by fixing a seed, using a single worker, or switching to a deterministic bounded optimizer as we did for the §`sec:oos` study) as a natural next step, but have chosen to disclose the instability honestly in this revision rather than quietly re-running until we got matching numbers, which would not address the underlying multimodality. As independent, quantitative corroboration that the underlying objective is genuinely multimodal rather than an artifact of these two specific runs, we reran the identical optimizer configuration at a reduced, computationally tractable scale (2×10⁷ steps vs. 10¹⁰, smaller population) under five explicit fixed seeds (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 4): the fitted k1 ranged from 9.33 to 14.26 (mean 12.43, sd 1.74) across the five seeds. We are explicit in the manuscript that these reduced-scale k1 values are not numerically comparable to the 6.481/8.070 pair, since reducing the step count changes the objective itself rather than only its cost — this experiment supports the general diagnosis (an unseeded population-based optimizer on this objective class will not reproduce a single answer run-to-run), not a specific reconciliation of the two original numbers.

**7. Contradictory large-N scaling protocol description (Results says single-point anchoring only; Appendix mentions multi-point/piecewise scaling).**
We have reviewed this and believe the apparent contradiction is a scope confusion that the manuscript should state more clearly, though we have only partially fixed it. §`sec:spectral-fitting-methodology` (Methods) now states explicitly: "We use this strict single-point anchoring throughout, including for the N=1000 dynamical fits (M1, M2) reported in the Macroscopic Regime section... The only exception is the GUE random-matrix baseline, which... is instead calibrated with a global least-squares scaling forced through the origin." The piecewise Shift-and-Invert method described in Appendix F.3 is a separate technique for a different, larger regime (N>10,000, used to extract eigenvalue *bands* without scanning the full spectrum) and does not change how the scaling constant a is determined once eigenphases are obtained — but the manuscript does not yet cross-reference this explicitly at the point the reviewer would read it (Appendix F.3 itself), so we acknowledge the two sections read as contradictory without that added cross-reference and have not yet added it.

**8. GUE baseline is not a like-for-like test.**
Fixed. §`sec:gue-comparison` now states the globally-rescaled-GUE-vs-counting-function result "is expected and is *not* a refutation of random-matrix theory," and adds the like-for-like test the reviewer requests: we unfolded our own model's eigenphases and the true zeros and ran a KS test against GUE and Poisson nearest-neighbor spacing distributions. Result: the true zeros match GUE (D=0.067, p=0.74) as expected; our model's own eigenphases do not (D=0.300, p<10⁻⁴) and are statistically indistinguishable from Poisson (D=0.082, p=0.49). We report this as "a genuine limitation of the numerical correspondence that we report directly rather than omit," concluding that our numerical match concerns mean/counting-function behavior, not the fine-grained local statistics that are the actual established GUE property.

**9. Post-hoc ion-trap comparison lacks a null distribution/significance test.**
Fixed with the significance testing described above in our response to Reviewer 58231421 Comment 3: §`sec:n20-significance` reports a Spearman rank correlation (ρ=0.56, p=0.010) and a 100,000-resample permutation test for the joint top-rank coincidence specifically at N=20 (p=0.099). We present both figures and do not claim the joint coincidence is established at conventional significance; the text states "we reiterate that even a significant rank coincidence would establish a shared statistical pattern, not a shared physical mechanism."

**10. Spectral filtering/unwrapping choices need sensitivity analysis.**
Addressed with a new sensitivity sweep (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 1), and the result is that the procedure is considerably less robust than the previous round's disclosure implied. Holding one empirical matrix fixed (the Strategy-C construction at k1=6.481, 10¹⁰ steps), we varied the magnitude cutoff (0.2–0.6), the conjugate-branch selection (upper half-plane, lower half-plane, or folded), and whether the filtered phases are sorted before unwrapping. The baseline configuration used in the main text (cutoff 0.4, upper half-plane, sorted) gives MSE≈288 against the comparison set used in this sweep; switching only the sort-before-unwrap step to "unsorted" inflates the MSE by roughly 20–40× and can flip the sign of the fitted slope, and switching only the branch to the lower half-plane produces a comparably large degradation. The cutoff value itself matters far less over the tested range than the ordering and branch choices do. Module C's existing disclosure ("we have not established its stability...") is now followed by these concrete numbers, and we no longer describe this only as an unverified assumption — it is now a verified, and substantial, robustness failure that we report as such rather than downplay.

**11. Numerical sensitivity (10⁻¹⁵ perturbations) conflicts with robustness claims.**
Partially addressed. We did add multi-seed robustness testing for the macroscopic k1 fit (§`sec:oos`: 10 seeds, CV≈4.7%) and repeated-trial testing for the ARPACK eigensolver (§`sec:n20-significance`: 20 trials). We have not, however, run the specific floating-point/compiler-sensitivity test the reviewer asks for (reproducing across compiler settings/precision levels for the full 10¹⁰-step macroscopic trajectories) — Appendix F.1 documents the sensitivity as a known issue and recommends a mitigation (isolating boundary-anchor constants in IEEE-754-compliant code), but does not report a systematic sweep across settings; given the other findings below, we have prioritized limited follow-up effort there over this specific sweep and still regard it as open. This, combined with the unseeded-DE instability disclosed above (Comment 6, now with independent multi-seed corroboration) and the new spectral-filtering sensitivity result (Comment 10), means the reviewer's underlying concern — that a single highly sensitive trajectory cannot support strong robustness claims — is one we now substantiate with concrete numbers rather than dispute, and Table `tab:claims` reflects this by marking the relevant claims as run-to-run variable or refuted rather than established.

# Appendix D: Response to Reviewer (Report 58279672)


We thank the reviewer for an unusually detailed and mathematically precise critique, including the direct algebraic argument in Concern 6, which we have adopted in full, and the identification of specific line numbers and inconsistent numerical values. **1. "Spectral isomorphism" is neither defined nor proved; the postulated unitary operator U and the empirical matrix P are never related.**

**2. The time-averaged matrix does not represent the spectrum of the non-autonomous evolution (ordered product vs. arithmetic mean); Eq. (10)'s Pij is a joint frequency, not a conditional probability.**

On the ordered product: Module B (Methods) now states verbatim the reviewer's own objection: "in general, the spectrum of this ordered product is unrelated to the spectrum of the arithmetic mean P̄_T=(1/T)Σ_t P_t, since averaging discards the temporal ordering and any non-commutative effects between the P_t. We do not provide an adiabatic theorem, ergodic argument, or convergence proof that would justify treating P̄_T as equivalent to the ordered-product spectrum; we instead treat P̄_T as a computationally tractable heuristic surrogate." This is a concession, not a resolution — we have not supplied the missing theorem, and do not believe we can within the scope of this study. On Pij: the reviewer is correct that the un-normalized joint frequency does not row-sum to 1. The equation now shown (labeled `eq:pij`) is the correctly normalized conditional frequency (divided by the source-bin occupation frequency, so Σ_j P_ij=1), with an added note: "the un-normalized joint frequency... appeared in place of Equation~\eqref{eq:pij} in an earlier draft of this manuscript; the implementation used to generate all numerical results reported below always applies the row normalization shown here, and the discrepancy was confined to the typeset equation, not the underlying code." We recognize this claim (code was always correct, only the typeset equation was wrong) is asserted rather than independently auditable by the reviewer from the paper alone; the released repository code is the verifiable record.

**3. The 1/ln²n law is imposed from target asymptotics, not derived; "renormalization flow"/"running coupling"/"ultraviolet cutoff" terminology is unsupported by a defined RG transformation.**

The scaling-law subsection is retitled "Heuristic Motivation for the Scaling Law" and now states the chain of correspondences "motivates, but does not derive from first principles" the leading-order form, with an explicit concession at the end: "We do not claim this constitutes a proof that such alternative forms are excluded on independent theoretical grounds, only that 1/ln²n performs better empirically among the forms we tried." **4. Substantial target leakage (μc anchored to first zero; ε selected via ErrSum against first six zeros; k1/k2 optimized against the full target spectrum at every scale; no genuine out-of-sample test).**
This is the central methodological gap, and we agree with the reviewer's diagnosis in full, including that a real fix requires fixing the complete model on one training interval and predicting a disjoint interval with no re-optimization. We implemented exactly this for the macroscopic k1 fit: §`sec:oos`, "Out-of-Sample Validation and Multi-Seed Robustness," fits k1 on the first M∈{50,70,80} zeros only and evaluates, without retuning, on the remaining zeros. The result confirms the reviewer's expectation of leakage rather than refuting it: held-out MSE is one to two orders of magnitude larger than training MSE at every M, and the fitted k1 itself drifts with M rather than converging (5.79→9.15→10.04). We state the conclusion directly: "the model is a calibrated in-sample reconstruction over the zeros it was fitted to, not a predictor of unseen zeros, and we do not claim otherwise." We have now extended this protocol to ε as well (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 3), and it reinforces the reviewer's diagnosis rather than qualifying it: at a reduced-cost pipeline scale, training ε on the first 3 of the first 6 zeros and testing on the last 3 gives a held-out error sum of 28.0, versus 5.4 when ε is instead chosen using all 6 zeros (the protocol reported in the main text) and evaluated on the same held-out zeros — a roughly 5× degradation that is direct, quantitative evidence of the leakage the reviewer identified. (A second, odd/even split happened to select the same ε as the full-sample optimum and so did not show the effect; we report both splits.) We have still **not** extended this protocol to μc or to the separately-refit N=1000/10000 regimes — the reviewer is correct that those remain target-leaked by the same standard, and we do not claim otherwise; Table `tab:claims` marks the low-order in-sample result as such, and we have not relabeled any N=1000/10000 result as "predictive."

**5. Phase-unwrapping is not well defined for an unordered matrix spectrum; the result depends on ordering/cutoff/branch choice with no stability analysis.**
We agree with the mathematical point, and have now run the sensitivity analysis this concern calls for (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 1); the result confirms the reviewer's expectation of instability rather than allaying it. Holding one empirical matrix fixed, we varied the magnitude cutoff (0.2–0.6), the conjugate-branch selection (upper half-plane, lower half-plane, or folded), and whether the filtered phases are sorted before unwrapping. The main text's configuration (cutoff 0.4, upper half-plane, sorted) gives MSE≈288 in this comparison; switching only the sort-before-unwrap step to "unsorted" inflates the MSE by roughly 20–40× and can flip the sign of the fitted slope, and switching only the branch to the lower half-plane produces a comparably large degradation, while the cutoff value itself matters far less over the tested range. Module C (Methods) now reports these numbers alongside its existing disclosure, so the instability the reviewer identified is quantified rather than only flagged as a possibility.

**6. The vanishing intercept is an algebraic consequence of symmetry; consequently Strategies C and D — if they differ only by mirroring the data — should produce equivalent slopes, and the fact that they don't indicates something else changed.**
We verified the reviewer's general algebraic claim numerically (constrained-origin OLS on positive-only data vs. `np.polyfit` on the symmetrized data, holding the underlying x-values fixed, does give identical slopes) and confirm it is correct as a general statement about regression. We now state the b=0 result exactly as the reviewer describes it: "it follows directly from the normal equations of ordinary least squares that the unconstrained intercept must satisfy b=0; this part of the result is therefore an algebraic consequence of the symmetric construction, not an independent finding about the dynamics" (§`sec:conjugate-ablation`). On why C and D's slopes nonetheless differ (6.48 vs. 4.39): the reviewer's expectation of equivalence would hold if C and D used the same underlying eigenphase data and only changed the regression. They do not: k1 is independently re-optimized for each strategy and enters the map (Eq.~\eqref{eq:pij}) before the transfer matrix P and its eigenphases are computed.
