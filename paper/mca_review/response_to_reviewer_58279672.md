# Response to Reviewer (Report 58279672, mca-4536128 (previously mca-4499257), Recommendation: Reject)

We thank the reviewer for an unusually detailed and mathematically precise critique, including the direct algebraic argument in Concern 6, which we have adopted in full, and the identification of specific line numbers and inconsistent numerical values. We address each concern in order. References are to the revised `main.tex`.

**1. "Spectral isomorphism" is neither defined nor proved; the postulated unitary operator U and the empirical matrix P are never related.**
Agreed, and we have removed the claim rather than attempting to supply the missing proof, which we cannot. The title no longer contains "isomorphism" (now "Numerical Spectral Correspondence... An Exploratory Study"). §`sec:model` (which the reviewer cites as Section 2.1) no longer postulates a unitary operator U at all; it opens instead with "We consider a classical, deterministic non-autonomous dynamical system on the interval [-1,1]; no Hilbert-space operator or unitary evolution is postulated or constructed at any point in this work." §`sec:rg-caveat` states directly: "We do not claim any isomorphism, in the precise mathematical sense of a structure-preserving bijection between independently defined objects, between this matrix and the zeros; the correspondence we report is a fitted numerical one." We agree this leaves the paper without the Hilbert–Pólya-type result its earlier framing implied; that is now the manuscript's explicit position rather than an unmet aspiration.

**2. The time-averaged matrix does not represent the spectrum of the non-autonomous evolution (ordered product vs. arithmetic mean); Eq. (10)'s Pij is a joint frequency, not a conditional probability.**
Both points accepted as correct critiques of the original text, and both are now addressed — the ordered-product point by explicit disclosure (we cannot resolve it, only disclose it), the normalization point by an actual fix. On the ordered product: Module B (Methods) now states verbatim the reviewer's own objection: "in general, the spectrum of this ordered product is unrelated to the spectrum of the arithmetic mean P̄_T=(1/T)Σ_t P_t, since averaging discards the temporal ordering and any non-commutative effects between the P_t. We do not provide an adiabatic theorem, ergodic argument, or convergence proof that would justify treating P̄_T as equivalent to the ordered-product spectrum; we instead treat P̄_T as a computationally tractable heuristic surrogate." This is a concession, not a resolution — we have not supplied the missing theorem, and do not believe we can within the scope of this study. On Pij: the reviewer is correct that the un-normalized joint frequency does not row-sum to 1. The equation now shown (labeled `eq:pij`) is the correctly normalized conditional frequency (divided by the source-bin occupation frequency, so Σ_j P_ij=1), with an added note: "the un-normalized joint frequency... appeared in place of Equation~\eqref{eq:pij} in an earlier draft of this manuscript; the implementation used to generate all numerical results reported below always applies the row normalization shown here, and the discrepancy was confined to the typeset equation, not the underlying code." We recognize this claim (code was always correct, only the typeset equation was wrong) is asserted rather than independently auditable by the reviewer from the paper alone; the released repository code is the verifiable record.

**3. The 1/ln²n law is imposed from target asymptotics, not derived; "renormalization flow"/"running coupling"/"ultraviolet cutoff" terminology is unsupported by a defined RG transformation.**
Agreed on both counts. The scaling-law subsection is retitled "Heuristic Motivation for the Scaling Law" and now states the chain of correspondences "motivates, but does not derive from first principles" the leading-order form, with an explicit concession at the end: "We do not claim this constitutes a proof that such alternative forms are excluded on independent theoretical grounds, only that 1/ln²n performs better empirically among the forms we tried." On RG terminology: §`sec:rg-caveat` states "we do not identify grid resolution with a renormalization-group ultraviolet cutoff, and we do not claim that this drift constitutes evidence of a renormalization-group flow in the dynamical sense (no beta function, fixed point, or universality result is established here)." We believe we have removed "Wilsonian," "running coupling," and "ultraviolet cutoff" as asserted physics claims; the parameter shift with resolution is now presented only as "an empirical observation about how the fit changes with grid resolution and range," alongside an explicit acknowledgment that "we cannot rule out that some part of this shift reflects overfitting to the specific fitting range used at each scale."

**4. Substantial target leakage (μc anchored to first zero; ε selected via ErrSum against first six zeros; k1/k2 optimized against the full target spectrum at every scale; no genuine out-of-sample test).**
This is the central methodological gap, and we agree with the reviewer's diagnosis in full, including that a real fix requires fixing the complete model on one training interval and predicting a disjoint interval with no re-optimization. We implemented exactly this for the macroscopic k1 fit: §`sec:oos`, "Out-of-Sample Validation and Multi-Seed Robustness," fits k1 on the first M∈{50,70,80} zeros only and evaluates, without retuning, on the remaining zeros. The result confirms the reviewer's expectation of leakage rather than refuting it: held-out MSE is one to two orders of magnitude larger than training MSE at every M, and the fitted k1 itself drifts with M rather than converging (5.79→9.15→10.04). We state the conclusion directly: "the model is a calibrated in-sample reconstruction over the zeros it was fitted to, not a predictor of unseen zeros, and we do not claim otherwise." We have now extended this protocol to ε as well (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 3), and it reinforces the reviewer's diagnosis rather than qualifying it: at a reduced-cost pipeline scale, training ε on the first 3 of the first 6 zeros and testing on the last 3 gives a held-out error sum of 28.0, versus 5.4 when ε is instead chosen using all 6 zeros (the protocol reported in the main text) and evaluated on the same held-out zeros — a roughly 5× degradation that is direct, quantitative evidence of the leakage the reviewer identified. (A second, odd/even split happened to select the same ε as the full-sample optimum and so did not show the effect; we report both splits.) We have still **not** extended this protocol to μc or to the separately-refit N=1000/10000 regimes — the reviewer is correct that those remain target-leaked by the same standard, and we do not claim otherwise; Table `tab:claims` marks the low-order in-sample result as such, and we have not relabeled any N=1000/10000 result as "predictive."

**5. Phase-unwrapping is not well defined for an unordered matrix spectrum; the result depends on ordering/cutoff/branch choice with no stability analysis.**
We agree with the mathematical point, and have now run the sensitivity analysis this concern calls for (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 1); the result confirms the reviewer's expectation of instability rather than allaying it. Holding one empirical matrix fixed, we varied the magnitude cutoff (0.2–0.6), the conjugate-branch selection (upper half-plane, lower half-plane, or folded), and whether the filtered phases are sorted before unwrapping. The main text's configuration (cutoff 0.4, upper half-plane, sorted) gives MSE≈288 in this comparison; switching only the sort-before-unwrap step to "unsorted" inflates the MSE by roughly 20–40× and can flip the sign of the fitted slope, and switching only the branch to the lower half-plane produces a comparably large degradation, while the cutoff value itself matters far less over the tested range. Module C (Methods) now reports these numbers alongside its existing disclosure, so the instability the reviewer identified is quantified rather than only flagged as a possibility.

**6. The vanishing intercept is an algebraic consequence of symmetry; consequently Strategies C and D — if they differ only by mirroring the data — should produce equivalent slopes, and the fact that they don't indicates something else changed.**
We verified the reviewer's general algebraic claim numerically (constrained-origin OLS on positive-only data vs. `np.polyfit` on the symmetrized data, holding the underlying x-values fixed, does give identical slopes) and confirm it is correct as a general statement about regression. We now state the b=0 result exactly as the reviewer describes it: "it follows directly from the normal equations of ordinary least squares that the unconstrained intercept must satisfy b=0; this part of the result is therefore an algebraic consequence of the symmetric construction, not an independent finding about the dynamics" (§`sec:conjugate-ablation`). On why C and D's slopes nonetheless differ (6.48 vs. 4.39): the reviewer's expectation of equivalence would hold if C and D used the same underlying eigenphase data and only changed the regression. They do not: k1 is independently re-optimized for each strategy and enters the map (Eq.~\eqref{eq:pij}) before the transfer matrix P and its eigenphases are computed. Strategy C's positive-branch eigenphases at its own optimal k1≈6.48 are therefore numerically distinct from Strategy D's positive-branch eigenphases at its own optimal k1≈4.39 — not a mirrored copy of one fixed data set under two different regressions. We say this directly in the text and add the reviewer's own counterfactual explicitly: "Had we instead fixed k1 at a single value and only changed whether the regression included the mirrored branch, the reviewer's expectation that the intercept-zero slope on the positive branch and the free-fit slope on the symmetrized branch coincide would apply exactly, by the same normal-equations argument used above for b=0; the observed slope change here reflects that we do not hold k1 fixed between strategies, not a departure from that identity." We regard this as answering the specific concern (identifying "what else changed") rather than disputing the reviewer's algebra.

**7. Numerical results are internally inconsistent between Table 1, Figure 4, and Figure 5.**
Confirmed and traced to its source, not merely disclaimed. As reported to other reviewers raising the same point: the two sets of numbers come from two separate runs of the same *unseeded* `differential_evolution` call for Strategy C (no fixed seed, `workers=-1` parallel population search), which converged to different local optima of a non-convex, multimodal objective (k1≈6.481, MSE≈8.48 vs. k1≈8.070, MSE≈10.08). This is exactly the kind of reproducibility failure the reviewer's Concern 11 (sensitivity) predicts, and we now disclose it as such rather than picking one number: Table 1's caption states this is "a genuine run-to-run optimizer instability, not evidence of two distinct experimental protocols," the Figure 4 caption states which run its numbers come from and cross-references Table 1's disclosure, and Figure 5's caption does the same. We added a corresponding "Refuted" row to Table `tab:claims`. We have not eliminated the instability (e.g., by seeding the optimizer) in this revision; we chose to document it transparently first, consistent with the reviewer's broader point that a highly sensitive, unreproduced computation should not be presented as settled. As independent corroboration that this is a genuine property of the objective and not a one-off pair of runs, we reran the identical optimizer configuration at a reduced, tractable scale under five explicit fixed seeds (§`sec:post-review-checks`, item 4): the fitted k1 ranged from 9.33 to 14.26 (mean 12.43, sd 1.74). We are explicit that these reduced-scale values are not numerically comparable to the original 6.481/8.070 pair, since reducing the step count changes the objective, not just its cost; the experiment supports the general diagnosis of multimodality, not a specific reconciliation of the two figures.

**8. Physical comparisons (ion-trap error features, GUE) are speculative and do not validate the mechanism.**
Agreed that these should not be read as validation, and the text is revised to make that explicit rather than implicit. On the ion-trap comparison: we added the significance testing described in our response to the parallel comments from the other reviewers on this point: §`sec:n20-significance` reports a Spearman correlation (ρ=0.56, p=0.010) and, specifically for the joint-top-rank coincidence at N=20 that motivates the qualitative comparison, a permutation test giving p=0.099 — not significant at the conventional threshold. We state: "even a significant rank coincidence would establish a shared statistical pattern, not a shared physical mechanism." On GUE: §`sec:gue-comparison` now states the asymmetric comparison "is expected and is *not* a refutation of random-matrix theory," and we added the like-for-like unfolded-local-statistics KS test the reviewer's own argument calls for: our model's own eigenphases fail to match GUE spacing statistics (D=0.300, p<10⁻⁴) and are indistinguishable from Poisson, whereas the true zeros do match GUE as expected (D=0.067, p=0.74) — we report this as "a genuine limitation of the numerical correspondence," concluding the numerical match documented in this paper concerns mean/counting-function behavior, not the local spectral statistics that are the actual established property of the zeros.

### Terminology, presentation, and English

We removed the specific rhetorical/metaphorical terms quoted in the review ("ocean of chaos," "topological fossil," "single-sided universe," "missing negative energy half of the universe," "structural shock absorber," "ultimate solution," "spontaneous calibrator," "tofu-cutting effect," "explodes with a bang") and the non-standard terms ("Riemann manifold," "topological stiffness," "cooling inertia," "homomorphic mapping," "physical topology") — we have grepped the current manuscript for each of these strings and confirmed none remain. Specific substitutions made: "Linear Homomorphic Mapping" → "Linear Rescaling"; "particle-hole symmetry" → "conjugate symmetry (a consequence of P being a real-valued matrix)"; "global cooling inertia" → "global rate"; and, in the present revision round, the tripartite parameter decomposition previously labeled "Topology Maintenance" / "Primary Aging Mechanism" / "dynamic aging process" is now "Baseline Term" / "Leading Correction," removing the remaining anthropomorphic framing. We have not performed a full independent copy-edit pass for grammar and capitalization (e.g., the specific malformed sentence the reviewer quotes, "the topological stiffness is instantaneously challenges," referred to language that no longer appears since the underlying passage was rewritten, but we have not systematically re-checked every sentence in the manuscript for similar grammatical issues); we would welcome a further pass identifying any remaining instances in a subsequent round.

### Overall

We accept the reviewer's central diagnosis: this manuscript does not, and by its own design cannot, establish a spectral isomorphism or a Hilbert–Pólya-type operator, and several of its numerical validations were genuinely target-leaked, symmetry-forced, or non-reproducible as originally presented. Rather than arguing against this assessment, our revision's strategy has been to (a) remove the overclaiming language throughout, (b) run the additional statistical tests (out-of-sample split, multi-seed robustness, N=20 significance test, unfolded GUE/Poisson KS test) that directly probe the concerns raised, and (c) disclose rather than paper over the specific reproducibility failures the reviewer identified (Table 1/Figure 4–5 optimizer instability). In this revision round we also ran the phase-unwrapping sensitivity analysis (Concern 5) and the ε out-of-sample test (part of Concern 4) that the previous round had left open, plus a multi-seed re-run of the k1 optimizer (Concern 7) and a direct overlap check between the Gaussian-splatting and Monte Carlo constructions (raised by another reviewer). None of these came back favorably: the sensitivity analysis shows the phase-extraction procedure is highly non-robust, the ε holdout test shows concrete target leakage, the multi-seed re-run confirms a multimodal objective, and the overlap check did not find consistent eigenphases between the two constructions. We report all four as-is. The ordered-product-vs-average justification (Concern 2) and full elimination of target leakage from every regime (Concern 4, for μc and the N=1000/10000 regimes) remain open; we do not claim to have resolved them, only to have stated them honestly.
