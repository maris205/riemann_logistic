Finding a dynamical system whose spectrum corresponds to the non-trivial
zeros of the Riemann \(\text{ζ}\) function is a long-standing question in
mathematical physics. Building on our recent result that the symbolic
dynamics of the Logistic map at its band-merging point is isomorphic to
the prime sieve [Wang, \emph{Research in Mathematics} 13(1), 2026], we
study a discrete dynamical operator driven by a non-autonomous
logarithmic cooling schedule
(\(\text{μ}_{\text{n}}\text{∼1/}\text{ln}^{\text{2}}\text{n}\)) and
compare its spectrum numerically to the Riemann zeros. At low and
intermediate orders the model reproduces the first zeros with small
error and exhibits a change of behavior near \(\text{N}\text{≈20}\). We
observe that this feature coincides spatially with the enlarged
measurement deviations reported near the same orders in recent ion-trap
quantum-simulation experiments (e.g., USTC), and we discuss --- as a
hypothesis rather than a demonstrated mechanism --- whether part of that
deviation could reflect a single-sided phase-space truncation rather
than purely instrumental decoherence. Extending the evolution into the
macroscopic regime (\(\text{N}\text{≥1000}\)), an ablation comparison
shows that a globally scaled Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) surrogate
deviates systematically in this counting-function comparison, consistent
with the fact that a single global scale cannot match a logarithmically
varying mean density; restoring the conjugate (\(\text{±}\text{θ}\))
spectrum drives the fitted ground-state intercept to approximately zero.
A second-order term motivated by a renormalization-group running
coupling improves the match to the logarithmic densification of the
zeros. We present these results as numerical observations and heuristic
arguments rather than proofs, and we state explicitly which claims are
established, numerical, or conjectural. This offers a non-autonomous,
dissipative-dynamics perspective on the Hilbert--Pólya program,
complementary to static-Hamiltonian and random-matrix approaches.
